# SixLens Framework — Lens 1: Reasoning
## Comprehensive Theory Document

---

## 1. Definition

The Reasoning Lens measures how a student naturally thinks and solves problems — not what they know, but how their mind processes information when working something out. It is the foundation lens because every other lens (exploring careers, understanding oneself, planning action, finding purpose) still requires the ability to reason through options and consequences.

**Core question:** *What am I naturally good at, and how do I think?*

---

## 2. Why this lens comes first

Career decisions are ultimately a sequence of reasoning tasks — comparing options, weighing trade-offs, predicting outcomes, and solving unfamiliar problems. A student's dominant reasoning style shapes which careers will feel natural versus which will feel like a constant uphill fight, regardless of interest. Placing this lens first grounds the rest of the framework in a realistic starting point rather than aspiration alone.

This lens is intentionally **descriptive, not evaluative** — there is no "better" or "worse" reasoning style. A student strong in spatial reasoning and weak in verbal reasoning is not less capable than one with the reverse profile; they are suited to different kinds of work.

---

## 3. The five reasoning dimensions

| Dimension | What it measures | Career relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Logical-Analytical** | Ability to identify patterns, sequences, and cause-effect relationships | Engineering, law, coding, research, strategy roles |
| **Numerical** | Comfort and accuracy working with quantities, data, and mathematical relationships | Finance, data science, actuarial work, economics |
| **Verbal-Linguistic** | Ability to understand, interpret, and construct language-based arguments | Writing, law, teaching, media, communications |
| **Spatial** | Ability to visualize, rotate, and manipulate objects or structures mentally | Architecture, design, surgery, engineering, animation |
| **Practical-Mechanical** | Ability to reason through how physical systems and processes work | Engineering trades, product design, operations, technical fields |

A student's profile is rarely flat — most will show two or three dominant dimensions. The assessment tool should surface this as a *profile*, not a single score.

---

## 4. Theoretical grounding

The Reasoning Lens draws loosely on two established traditions, reframed in accessible language for students and parents:

- **Multiple Intelligences theory (Gardner)** — the idea that "intelligence" is not one general trait but several distinct capacities. This lens narrows Gardner's broader framework to the dimensions most relevant to career reasoning specifically (logical, linguistic, spatial), rather than the full set (which includes musical, interpersonal, etc. — covered partially by later lenses).
- **Classical Nyaya reasoning structure** — the SixLens methodology's own philosophical root treats reasoning as a disciplined process: observation, inference, comparison, and testimony as valid means of knowledge (*pramanas*). Practically, this shows up in the assessment as questions that test not just "can the student get the right answer" but "what method did they use to get there" — surfacing reasoning *style*, not just correctness. (This grounding is used to inform question design; it is not surfaced to students/parents directly — see Step 6 of the build plan.)

---

## 5. What this lens deliberately does NOT measure

- **Academic performance or marks** — a student can reason strongly in a dimension and still underperform in school due to teaching style, language of instruction, or test anxiety. This lens looks at raw reasoning capacity, independent of school grades.
- **Interest or motivation** — a student might reason well numerically but have no interest in finance. That gap is intentional; interest is addressed in the Self-Understanding lens, not here.
- **Speed alone** — while some timing is used to keep the assessment practical, the tool should weight accuracy and approach over raw speed, since real-world reasoning tasks rarely reward speed above correctness.

---

## 6. How this lens feeds into the rest of the framework

- Feeds **Awareness Lens**: reasoning profile narrows which career categories are worth exploring in depth first.
- Feeds **Discipline Lens**: a student's weaker dimensions become explicit skill-building targets in the action plan, rather than being ignored.
- Provides a shared, neutral vocabulary for the parent session — "your child reasons strongly through spatial and practical-mechanical thinking" is a much easier conversation starter than "your child isn't good at maths."

---

## 7. Output format for students/parents

The assessment should never return a single number or percentile. Output should be a **profile summary**, e.g.:

> *Your strongest reasoning dimensions are Spatial and Practical-Mechanical, with moderate strength in Logical-Analytical. This profile is common among students who thrive in hands-on, visual, or systems-based fields — such as engineering, architecture, product design, or technical trades. Your Verbal-Linguistic dimension is comparatively lighter, which is useful to know but not limiting.*

This framing keeps the tone strengths-based, which matters given how much test anxiety already exists around "aptitude tests" in the Indian school context.
